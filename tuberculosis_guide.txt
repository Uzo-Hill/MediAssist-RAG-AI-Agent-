
TUBERCULOSIS (TB): A COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL GUIDE

1. OVERVIEW
Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, primarily affecting the lungs.
It spreads through the air when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or speaks.
Africa carries approximately 25% of the global TB burden. Nigeria, South Africa,
and DR Congo are among the highest-burden countries.

2. TYPES
Latent TB: Bacteria present but inactive. No symptoms, not contagious.
Active TB: Bacteria active, causing illness. Infectious and can spread to others.
Pulmonary TB: Affects lungs. Most common and most contagious form.
Extrapulmonary TB: Affects lymph nodes, bones, kidneys, brain, or heart lining.
MDR-TB: Resistant to Isoniazid and Rifampicin. Harder to treat.

3. SYMPTOMS
Pulmonary TB:
- Persistent cough lasting 2 weeks or more, often with blood-stained sputum
- Chest pain and difficulty breathing
- Fever especially in evenings
- Night sweats
- Unexplained weight loss and loss of appetite
- Extreme fatigue

Extrapulmonary symptoms vary by organ:
- TB lymphadenitis: Swollen neck lymph nodes
- TB meningitis: Severe headache, stiff neck, confusion
- Skeletal TB: Back pain, joint swelling

4. RISK FACTORS
- HIV infection (18x higher risk)
- Close contact with active TB case
- Overcrowded, poorly ventilated conditions
- Malnutrition and poverty
- Diabetes mellitus
- Smoking and excessive alcohol

5. DIAGNOSIS
- Sputum Smear Microscopy: Quick, widely available in Africa
- GeneXpert/Xpert MTB/RIF: Detects TB and rifampicin resistance in 2 hours
- Chest X-ray: Shows characteristic TB lung patterns
- Culture: Most sensitive but takes 4-6 weeks

6. TREATMENT
Standard 6-month course:
- Intensive Phase (2 months): Isoniazid + Rifampicin + Pyrazinamide + Ethambutol
- Continuation Phase (4 months): Isoniazid + Rifampicin
Treatment is free in most African countries through national TB programs.

MDR-TB: 9-18 months with Bedaquiline, Linezolid, Levofloxacin.
Directly Observed Therapy (DOT): Healthcare worker watches each dose.

7. TB AND HIV
- Sub-Saharan Africa has highest TB-HIV co-infection burden globally
- All TB patients should be tested for HIV and vice versa
- TB is the leading cause of death among people living with HIV in Africa

8. PREVENTION
- BCG vaccination at birth (protects against severe childhood TB)
- TB Preventive Therapy (TPT): Isoniazid for 6 months for high-risk groups
- Improve ventilation in homes, schools, and health facilities
- Early diagnosis and treatment of active cases

9. GLOBAL STATISTICS
- 10.6 million TB cases globally in 2022 (WHO)
- 1.3 million deaths from TB in 2022
- Africa accounts for 23% of cases and 31% of deaths

10. WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Cough lasting 2 weeks or more, especially with blood in sputum
- Unexplained weight loss with fever and night sweats
- Known contact with a TB patient
- HIV-positive individuals should screen for TB at every healthcare visit
