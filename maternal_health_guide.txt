
MATERNAL HEALTH: A COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL GUIDE

1. OVERVIEW
Maternal health covers the health of women during pregnancy, childbirth, and postnatal period.
Sub-Saharan Africa accounts for approximately 70% of global maternal deaths. The maternal
mortality ratio in sub-Saharan Africa is approximately 545 deaths per 100,000 live births,
compared to a global average of 223 per 100,000 live births.

2. ANTENATAL CARE
WHO recommends at least 8 contacts during pregnancy:
- First contact before 12 weeks
- Additional contacts at 20, 26, 30, 34, 36, 38, and 40 weeks

Key components:
- Blood pressure monitoring (detects pre-eclampsia)
- Blood tests for anemia, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B
- Urine testing for protein and infection
- Ultrasound to confirm gestational age
- Nutritional supplementation: Iron, folic acid, calcium
- Malaria prevention: Bed nets and IPTp
- Tetanus vaccination
- Birth planning and emergency preparedness

3. DANGER SIGNS DURING PREGNANCY
Seek immediate care if you experience:
- Severe headache with visual disturbances (pre-eclampsia sign)
- Swelling of face, hands, and legs
- Vaginal bleeding at any stage
- Reduced or absent fetal movements after 28 weeks
- High fever
- Severe abdominal pain
- Water breaking before labor begins
- Difficulty breathing

4. MAJOR CAUSES OF MATERNAL DEATH IN AFRICA
- Haemorrhage (27%): Excessive bleeding before or after delivery
- Hypertensive disorders (14%): Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia
- Infections and sepsis (11%)
- Unsafe abortion complications (8%)
- Obstructed labor
- Severe anaemia

5. PRE-ECLAMPSIA AND ECLAMPSIA
Pre-eclampsia: Blood pressure above 140/90 mmHg after 20 weeks plus protein in urine.
Eclampsia: Pre-eclampsia progressing to seizures — life-threatening.
Treatment: Magnesium sulphate for seizures, antihypertensives for blood pressure,
delivery of baby as soon as mother is stabilized.

6. POSTNATAL CARE
Most critical period: First 24 hours after delivery.
Postnatal contacts: Within 24 hours, 3 days, 1-2 weeks, and 6 weeks after delivery.
Monitor for: Postpartum haemorrhage, infection, postpartum depression.

7. SKILLED BIRTH ATTENDANCE
- All deliveries should be attended by a skilled health professional
- Only 59% of births in sub-Saharan Africa attended by skilled personnel
- Facility-based deliveries have significantly lower mortality rates

8. MATERNAL NUTRITION
- Iron and folic acid supplementation as early as possible in pregnancy
- Calcium supplementation reduces pre-eclampsia risk
- Avoid alcohol, tobacco, and traditional herbal remedies during pregnancy

9. WHEN TO SEEK CARE
During pregnancy: At first sign of pregnancy and for any danger signs above.
After delivery: Heavy bleeding, fever above 38°C, severe abdominal pain,
signs of postpartum depression.
