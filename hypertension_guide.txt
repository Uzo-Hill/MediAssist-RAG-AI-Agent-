
HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE): A COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL GUIDE

1. OVERVIEW
Hypertension is a chronic condition where blood pressure is consistently too high —
defined as systolic 140 mmHg or higher, or diastolic 90 mmHg or higher. Called the
silent killer because it typically has no symptoms. In Africa, it affects approximately
30% of adults and is the leading cause of stroke and cardiovascular disease.

2. CLASSIFICATION
- Normal: Below 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: Systolic 120-129 mmHg AND Diastolic below 80 mmHg
- Stage 1: Systolic 130-139 OR Diastolic 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2: Systolic 140+ OR Diastolic 90+ mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: Above 180/120 mmHg (emergency)

3. SYMPTOMS
Usually none. When present:
- Severe headache at back of head in the morning
- Nosebleeds
- Shortness of breath
- Blurred vision
- Chest pain or palpitations
- Dizziness

4. RISK FACTORS
Non-modifiable: Age, family history, African ethnicity (higher rates and severity)
Modifiable: Obesity, physical inactivity, high salt diet, excessive alcohol,
smoking, chronic stress, diabetes, chronic kidney disease

5. DIAGNOSIS
- Two or more elevated readings on two or more separate visits
- Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (24-hour)
- Home blood pressure monitoring for ongoing management

6. TREATMENT
Lifestyle (first line for Stage 1):
- Reduce salt to less than 5 grams per day
- DASH diet (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy)
- Exercise 150 minutes moderate activity per week
- Achieve healthy body weight, limit alcohol, stop smoking

Medications:
- ACE Inhibitors (Lisinopril, Enalapril)
- ARBs (Losartan, Valsartan)
- Calcium Channel Blockers (Amlodipine)
- Diuretics (Hydrochlorothiazide)
- Beta-Blockers (Atenolol)
Most African patients require two or more medications.

7. COMPLICATIONS
- Stroke: Most important risk factor for stroke in Africa
- Heart attack and heart failure
- Kidney damage and chronic kidney disease
- Retinopathy (vision loss)
- Peripheral artery disease

8. STATISTICS IN AFRICA
- 130 million adults affected in sub-Saharan Africa
- Fewer than 40% know they have it
- Fewer than 10% achieve controlled blood pressure
- Salt intake far exceeds WHO recommended limits in many African countries

9. WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR
- Blood pressure above 140/90 on two separate occasions
- Sudden severe headache, chest pain, or shortness of breath with high BP
- Any stroke symptoms: face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty
