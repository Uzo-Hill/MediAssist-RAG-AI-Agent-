
TYPHOID FEVER: A COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL GUIDE

1. OVERVIEW
Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella typhi and spreads through contaminated food and water.
It is endemic across West Africa, including Nigeria, where inadequate water supply and
sanitation create widespread transmission. An estimated 11-21 million cases occur globally
each year, causing 128,000-161,000 deaths.

2. HOW IT SPREADS
- Drinking water contaminated with Salmonella typhi
- Eating food handled by infected person who did not wash hands
- Eating raw fruits or vegetables washed with contaminated water
- Eating shellfish from contaminated water
Carriers: Some people carry bacteria without symptoms and can spread typhoid to others.

3. SYMPTOMS
Week 1: Gradually rising fever (39-40°C), headache, weakness, dry cough, abdominal pain
Week 2: Sustained high fever, slow heartbeat despite fever, rose spots on abdomen,
enlarged spleen and liver, abdominal distension, diarrhoea or constipation
Week 3 (if untreated): Intestinal perforation, peritonitis, severe bleeding, delirium

4. DIAGNOSIS
- Blood Culture: Gold standard, most effective in first week
- Widal Test: Widely used in Africa but has high false positive rate
- Typhidot/Tubex Test: Rapid tests increasingly available
- Stool and Urine Culture: Useful in later stages

5. TREATMENT
- Third-generation Cephalosporins (Ceftriaxone, Cefixime): Current first-line
- Azithromycin: Effective especially for drug-resistant cases
- Fluoroquinolones: Widespread resistance now limits use
- Supportive care: Fluids, paracetamol for fever, rest, nutrition
- Surgery: Required for intestinal perforation

6. COMPLICATIONS
- Intestinal perforation (surgical emergency, up to 3% of cases)
- Intestinal bleeding
- Encephalopathy (brain dysfunction)
- Myocarditis (heart muscle inflammation)
- Relapse in 5-10% of patients after completing antibiotics

7. PREVENTION
- Safe water: Drink only boiled, filtered, or bottled water
- Food safety: Eat only thoroughly cooked food served hot
- Hand hygiene: Wash hands before eating and after toilet
- Sanitation: Proper sewage disposal infrastructure
- Vaccination:
  Vi polysaccharide vaccine (Typhim Vi): Single injection, 2-3 years protection
  Ty21a oral vaccine: 3-4 capsules on alternate days
  Typhoid Conjugate Vaccine (TCV): WHO recommended for children from 6 months

8. TYPHOID IN NIGERIA AND WEST AFRICA
- Nigeria has one of the highest typhoid burdens in Africa
- Contaminated water affects over 60% of the population
- Self-medication contributes significantly to drug resistance
- WHO recommends TCV introduction into routine immunization programs

9. WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Fever lasting more than 3 days with headache and abdominal pain
- Rose spots on the abdomen
- Severe abdominal pain (possible perforation — emergency)
- Return of fever after appearing to improve
Do not self-medicate — this causes drug resistance.
