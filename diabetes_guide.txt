
DIABETES: A COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL GUIDE

1. OVERVIEW
Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated levels of blood
glucose, which leads over time to serious damage to the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys,
and nerves. There are three main types: Type 1, Type 2, and Gestational diabetes.
Globally, an estimated 422 million people have diabetes, and 1.5 million deaths are directly
attributed to the disease each year.

2. TYPES OF DIABETES
Type 1 Diabetes: An autoimmune condition in which the body's immune system attacks and destroys
insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. It requires daily insulin administration and
typically develops in children and young adults.
Type 2 Diabetes: The most common form, accounting for around 90% of all diabetes cases.
It occurs when the body does not use insulin effectively. Strongly linked to obesity,
physical inactivity, and poor diet.
Gestational Diabetes: Develops during pregnancy and usually resolves after delivery.
However, it increases the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes later in life.

3. SYMPTOMS
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Extreme fatigue and lack of energy
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing of wounds and cuts
- Tingling, numbness, or pain in hands and feet (neuropathy)
- Recurrent infections such as skin, gum, or bladder infections

4. RISK FACTORS
- Being overweight or obese (BMI over 25)
- Physical inactivity and sedentary lifestyle
- Family history of diabetes
- Age over 45 years
- High blood pressure (hypertension)
- History of gestational diabetes
- Unhealthy diet high in sugar and processed foods

5. DIAGNOSIS
- Fasting Blood Glucose Test: A reading of 126 mg/dL or higher confirms diabetes
- HbA1c Test: An HbA1c level of 6.5% or above confirms diabetes
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test: A 2-hour blood glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher
- Random Blood Sugar Test: A reading of 200 mg/dL or higher with symptoms

6. TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT
Medication: Type 1 requires insulin. Type 2 managed with Metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors,
or GLP-1 receptor agonists.
Diet: Balanced diet low in refined sugars and processed foods.
Exercise: At least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week.
Monitoring: Regular blood glucose self-monitoring with a glucometer.

7. COMPLICATIONS
- Cardiovascular disease (2-3x higher risk)
- Diabetic nephropathy (kidney damage)
- Diabetic retinopathy (leading cause of blindness)
- Peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage)
- Diabetic foot and risk of amputation

8. PREVENTION
- Maintain a healthy body weight
- Follow a nutritious, balanced diet rich in fibre
- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol
- Get regular health screenings
- Manage blood pressure and cholesterol

9. GLOBAL STATISTICS
- 422 million people worldwide live with diabetes
- 1 in 10 adults globally has diabetes
- 1.5 million deaths directly caused by diabetes annually
- Over 80% of diabetes deaths in low and middle income countries
- Global healthcare cost exceeds $760 billion annually

10. WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR
- Persistent thirst or frequent urination
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue that does not improve with rest
- Blurred vision or slow-healing wounds
- Numbness or tingling in hands or feet
