
MALARIA: A COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL GUIDE

1. OVERVIEW
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites transmitted through
the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Africa accounts for over 90% of global
malaria deaths. Children under five and pregnant women are most vulnerable.

2. TYPES
Plasmodium falciparum: Most deadly, responsible for majority of African malaria deaths.
Plasmodium vivax: Less deadly but causes relapses months or years after initial infection.
Plasmodium malariae: Milder form but can persist for decades if untreated.
Plasmodium ovale: Rare, found mainly in West Africa.

3. SYMPTOMS
Early symptoms (10-15 days after bite):
- Fever and chills (cyclical, every 48-72 hours)
- Headache and body aches
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fatigue and weakness
- Sweating followed by return to normal temperature

Severe malaria (falciparum):
- Extreme weakness, inability to sit or walk
- Impaired consciousness or coma (cerebral malaria)
- Seizures
- Respiratory distress
- Jaundice (yellowing of skin and eyes)
- Dark or black urine (blackwater fever)

4. DIAGNOSIS
- Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs): Detect malaria antigens within 15 minutes
- Microscopy: Blood smear examination — gold standard
- GeneXpert: Detects TB DNA and rifampicin resistance within 2 hours
- PCR Testing: Highly sensitive molecular test

5. TREATMENT
Uncomplicated malaria: Artemisinin-based Combination Therapies (ACTs) — 3 day course
Severe malaria: IV/IM Artesunate — requires hospitalization
Plasmodium vivax: Chloroquine plus Primaquine (G6PD testing required first)

6. PREVENTION
- Insecticide-Treated Bed Nets (ITNs): Sleep under nets every night
- Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS): Insecticide on walls kills resting mosquitoes
- Seasonal Malaria Chemoprevention (SMC) for children
- Intermittent Preventive Treatment in Pregnancy (IPTp)
- RTS,S/AS01 (Mosquirix) and R21/Matrix-M vaccines approved by WHO
- Eliminate standing water where mosquitoes breed

7. GLOBAL STATISTICS
- 249 million malaria cases recorded globally in 2022 (WHO)
- Africa accounts for 94% of cases and 95% of deaths
- Children under 5 account for 80% of malaria deaths in Africa
- Nigeria, DRC, Uganda, Mozambique account for over 50% of global cases
- 608,000 deaths from malaria in 2022

8. WHEN TO SEEK CARE
- Fever above 38.5°C especially after being in a malaria area
- Fever with chills, headache, and body aches in a child under five
- Any symptoms of severe malaria listed above
Malaria can become life-threatening within hours — do not wait.
